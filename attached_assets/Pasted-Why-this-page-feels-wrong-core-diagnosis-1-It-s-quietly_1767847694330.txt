Why this page feels wrong (core diagnosis)
1. It’s quietly giving away answers

The moment you show rent ranges prominently on the market grid, you’ve already shifted the player’s brain into:

“Which one has the best-looking numbers?”

That short-circuits the lesson.

Right now this page is functioning like:

Zillow browsing

Deal teaser feed

“Pick the green one”

But your game is not about spotting green numbers.
It’s about building outcomes from incomplete inputs.

2. The “Good / Fair / Excellent” tags are doing too much thinking

Those labels feel comforting — and that’s the problem.

They imply:

someone else already evaluated this

the player is choosing between quality tiers

“Excellent” ≈ safe

In reality (and in your game design):

“Excellent” deals can still be lethal

“Fixer-Upper” can sometimes be the only viable one

Surface labels are often lies

So the UI is contradicting the philosophy.

3. This page feels like a selection screen, not a risk screen

Emotionally, this page says:

“Choose the best option”

But it should say:

“Choose where you’re willing to be wrong”

That’s a very different psychological posture.

The fix: change what the page teaches, not the whole layout

You do not need to redesign everything.
You need to change what information is foregrounded vs backgrounded.

What to REMOVE or de-emphasize
❌ Remove rent ranges from the card face

Rent ranges are inputs, but they’re high-leverage inputs.

If you show them here, players anchor on them.

Instead:

Move rent range to the property detail page only

Or hide it behind a hover / “Market Info” toggle

This forces players to:

click in

slow down

engage deliberately

That’s good friction.

❌ Kill “Excellent / Good / Fair” labels

These are poison for what you’re teaching.

Replace them with neutral uncertainty language.

What to ADD (this is the important part)
1. Replace quality labels with uncertainty indicators

Instead of:

Excellent

Good

Fair

Fixer-Upper

Use things like:

Information Level: Low / Medium / High

Condition Certainty: Unknown / Partial / Clear

Hidden Risk Likelihood: Low / Medium / High

These do not tell the player what’s good.
They tell the player how much they don’t know.

That aligns perfectly with pro forma thinking.

2. Replace rent ranges with questions

This is subtle but powerful.

Instead of showing:

$1,300–$1,600

Show something like:

“Market rent uncertain”

“Wide rent variance”

“Tight rent band”

Or even:

“Rent sensitivity: Medium”

You’re teaching sensitivity, not numbers.

3. Add a small line of instructional framing at the top

Right now the header says:

“Select a property to analyze”

That’s neutral — and boring.

Change it to something like:

“Which of these would you bet your assumptions on?”

or

“Most of these will fail. Choose carefully.”

That single sentence reframes the entire screen.

What this page SHOULD teach (explicitly)

This page’s lesson is:

“Before you build a pro forma, all deals look plausible.”

So the UI should:

make multiple options look tempting

hide decisive numbers

foreground uncertainty

invite investigation, not selection

A concrete revised card structure (example)

Card shows:

Photo

Price

Sq ft

Neighborhood descriptor

Uncertainty badges:

“Rent variability: High”

“Condition clarity: Low”

“Timeline risk: Unknown”

Card does NOT show:

Rent $

Cash flow $

Profit $

Quality verdicts

Clicking the card is how you earn more info.

Why this will feel better immediately

Players slow down

They stop shopping for numbers

They start asking questions

Pro forma becomes the hero again

Passing on deals feels smart, not like “missing out”

That’s exactly the behavior you want to train.
